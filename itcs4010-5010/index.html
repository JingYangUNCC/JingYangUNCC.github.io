ABOUT THE COURSE

People and AI, working as a team

This project-driven course explores how people and AI systems collaborate to gather information, analyze data, build and evaluate analytical tools, communicate findings, and make decisions. You’ll study what humans and AI each do well (and badly), build interactive AI-supported analysis applications, and develop provenance functions that make AI evidence, reliability, and risks transparent, so people can see where results came from and when a human needs to stay in charge.

Build

Create AI analysis systems with interactive visual interfaces.

Explore

Analyze documents, images, video, tables, and networks with AI as your partner.

Verify

Build provenance functions that trace where AI results come from, and catch hallucinations, bias, and privacy leaks.

Safeguard

Design checks, approval steps, and oversight that keep humans in control.

Orchestrate

Design and evaluate AI agent systems, including multiple agents working together, with humans approving key steps.

LEARNING OUTCOMES

By the end, you’ll be able to…

  • Explain different roles and divisions of responsibility between human analysts and AI systems.
  • Use AI tools to gather, organize, and critically assess information from documents and other sources, with traceable evidence.
  • Design and prototype interactive human–AI workflows for exploratory data analysis and communication.
  • Evaluate AI-generated outputs for correctness, uncertainty, provenance, usefulness, and reproducibility.
  • Investigate when and why risks such as hallucination, privacy leakage, multi-turn behavioral drift, bias, overreliance, and loss of human control occur, and develop methods to detect and prevent them.
  • Design and test safeguards such as evidence checks, risk monitoring, approval gates, and human oversight.
  • Design and evaluate agentic workflows, including multi-agent systems, that balance AI autonomy with human control.
  • Communicate research findings, document AI use and individual contributions, and collaborate constructively.

HOW IT WORKS

Learn by doing, not by cramming

There are no exams. Your grade comes from the work you build, test, discuss, and present.

Round-table discussions

Read and debate current research on human–AI collaboration and AI risks.

Hands-on projects

Build-with-AI assignments, plus a final team prototype that you evaluate and present.

Student presentations

Demo your tools, give peer feedback, and present your final project.

Analysis log

Keep a record of how you worked with AI and critically evaluate what it got right and wrong.

Undergraduate and graduate

Undergraduates (ITCS 4010) and graduate students (ITCS 5010) share the same sessions. Both levels do the same work; the two courses use different grading scales.

AI tools you’ll use

You can use free University-provided tools, such as Google Gemini or Microsoft Copilot, or paid subscriptions such as Claude or ChatGPT. There’s no textbook to buy.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need to know AI or programming already?

No. There are no prerequisites, and no prior experience with AI development or data visualization is required.

Are there exams?

No. You’re assessed on discussions, build-with-AI assignments, presentations (including a demo of your small interactive visual analytics tool), your analysis log, and a final collaborative project.

Can I use AI on assignments?

Yes. Using generative AI is a core part of the course, unless an assignment says otherwise. You disclose how you used it and you verify what it produces.

How is this different from Visual Analytics?

This course focuses on human–AI roles, workflow design, verification, and evaluation, not a full study of visualization encodings and dashboard programming.
